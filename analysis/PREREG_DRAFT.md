## Study question

How much feedback does a preference-driven adaptive UI need, and can new users start from the preferences of similar users?

## Hypotheses

- **H1** [confirm]: a personal model reaches 90% of a person's self-consistency ceiling well below 250 comparisons. This study can observe at most 50 training comparisons, so "well below 250" is assessed as the median k90 being at or below 50. [confirm this reading]
- **H2** [confirm]: a cluster start plus few comparisons matches scratch plus many comparisons. Assessed by comparing cluster_choice at low k with scratch at high k. [confirm which values of k count as "few" and "many"]
- **H3** [confirm]: clusters built from choices predict held-out users better than clusters built from questionnaire answers, tested as the cluster_choice vs cluster_questionnaire contrast at each k. In this one-group design, new users are held-out participants, not people recruited later.
- **H4a**: adaptive conditions satisfy users more than static, tested as each adaptive condition vs static.
- **H4b**: interfaces from cluster5 satisfy users as much as those from personalFull, tested for equivalence on QUIS.

There are no subgroup analyses. Background items (age, gender, occupation, technology use, reading glasses, reading language, CS/IT background and others) are described only.

## Design

One group of N adults aged 40 or over does Session A (comparison trials) and then Session B (4 grocery shopping tasks in differently adapted shops). Default N = 32, minimum 16. Every participant finishes Session A before anyone starts Session B. Eligibility (age 40+) is checked at the start of Session A; people under 40 are thanked and leave, and only their age and the screening result are kept.

Participants choose English or Urdu before consent; the whole study, including the clips and the shop, is in that language. Urdu is shown right to left.

The interface space has 7 settings and 432 configurations: text size (standard, large, extra large), target size (standard, large), button style (flat, outlined, raised), icon labels (icon only, icon and text), contrast (standard, high), layout (3-column grid, 2-column grid, list) and guidance (none, hints). The base is the first level of each.

Session A: 70 main trials, 10 repeats (sides swapped, at least 20 trials later) and 2 attention checks, plus practice trials. Each clip shows the base shop changing to a target config within one product category (the scene). Both clips of a trial share the scene and language; scenes are balanced within each participant (within 1), no scene runs more than 2 trials in a row, and a repeat keeps its original's scene.

Session B conditions, ordered by balanced Latin squares: static, global, cluster5 and personalFull. Each task is followed by QUIS and UES-SF, and the session ends with a ranking of the 4 interfaces.

## Exclusions

Every rule is reported with counts and is switchable in the analysis config.

- Practice trials are dropped from all analyses.
- Participants who fail an attention check are excluded from model building and from the primary analyses. They still receive a flagged plan, and a sensitivity analysis keeps them in.
- Responses under 300 ms are flagged and reported, not excluded.
- Withdrawn participants are excluded from the models and from Session B. Their withdrawal and reason are recorded.
- Someone who finishes Session A after the freeze is either excluded or receives a plan from the frozen models, flagged as late.

## Preference model

A Bradley-Terry / logistic model, fitted by MAP with a Gaussian prior w ~ N(mu, sigma^2 I), on 10 dummy features with base levels as reference. Nuisance terms: a global left-position bias and, because the clip set identifies it, a global busyness curvature. Configs are scored with w . x only.

A linear busyness term is not included: the number of changed settings is the exact sum of the 10 dummies, so it cannot be separated from the weights. The weights therefore include any general liking for more or less change, and the analysis reports how many targets would move under a range of assumed values.

Prior mean = starting point: 0 for scratch, pooled weights of everyone else for global, and pooled weights of the assigned cluster for the cluster starts.

sigma: one value for all starting points, chosen from [0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 3.0, 4.0, 6.0] by lowest held-out log-loss averaged over all starting points and k. The selection runs inside the other participants, so the held-out person has no influence on it.

## Learning-curve procedure and k90

For each participant: 20 main trials are drawn at random as a test set, and the remaining 50 stay in chronological order as the training pool. Models train on the first k = 5, 10, 20, 30, 50 comparisons. This is repeated with 10 random test splits and averaged.

k90 is the smallest k reaching 90% of a person's ceiling. Raw self-consistency (share of the 10 repeats where the same clip was chosen) is the primary ceiling. A corrected ceiling, (1 + sqrt(2c - 1)) / 2, is secondary, because a model can legitimately beat test-retest agreement. If 50 comparisons are not enough, k90 is recorded as ">50" and treated as censored. A participant whose 90% target is at or below chance is counted as "k90 not meaningful" and left out of the medians.

## Starting points and clusters

All comparisons are leave-one-out: for each participant, the nuisance terms, sigma, global model, clusters and cluster models are rebuilt without that participant.

- Choice-based clusters: k-means on each person's weights, with 2 or 3 clusters chosen by silhouette. A held-out person is assigned by likelihood, using only their first k training comparisons.
- Questionnaire clusters: k-means on the self-rated preference items with the same number of clusters. "No preference" is neutral. A held-out person is assigned from their answers alone, so this start also exists at k = 0.
- Fewer than 8 eligible participants: 1 cluster (cluster = global).

## Phase 3 conditions and frozen models

- static: no adaptation.
- global: the leave-one-out global model.
- cluster5: cluster assigned from the participant's first 5 main trials, then fine-tuned with those trials.
- personalFull: all their main trials, fitted from scratch.

Targets are restricted to configs with 2-4 changed settings from the base. Each adaptive condition applies one change, after 8000 ms, so only the target config differs.

An RL agent solves the adaptation MDP exactly by value iteration, and its result is checked against a brute-force search. The models are frozen once, after the last Session A, and never change after Session B begins.

## Statistical models and corrections

Learning curves: correct ~ start * log(k) + (1|participant) + (1|test_pair), a mixed-effects logistic model fitted once per test split, with estimates and standard errors averaged across splits. Pairwise contrasts at each k with Holm correction within k. Sensitivity: (start|participant). k = 0 baselines are compared separately, by participant bootstrap. Median k90 per starting point with bootstrap CIs.

Fatigue: consistent ~ half + trial_gap + (1|participant), mixed-effects logistic, with the first half being trials 1-41.

Phase 3: QUIS, UES-SF and log(task time): outcome ~ condition + position + task + language + (1|participant), with study language (English or Urdu) as a covariate; if everyone used one language the term is dropped. Task success (all 6 items found and within budget): the same structure, mixed-effects logistic. Items found (0-6) is secondary. Contrasts: each adaptive condition vs static, and cluster5 vs personalFull, with Holm across these 4 contrasts within each outcome. QUIS is the primary outcome. [confirm]

H4b: TOST for cluster5 vs personalFull on QUIS, using the mixed-model contrast and its CI, with an equivalence margin of ±0.5 scale points, fixed before Phase 3 data exists. The 90% CI and both one-sided tests are reported. [confirm the margin once the licensed QUIS scale is in]

Rankings: Friedman test, pairwise Wilcoxon with Holm correction, mean rank per condition and a Plackett-Luce model.

Sensitivity analyses: attention failures kept in, and participants with identical cluster5 and personalFull targets left out.

Nuisance check (descriptive): share of Option A, median response time and agreement with each person's own weights, by scene and by language.
